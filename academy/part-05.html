01

The Electrical System

The electrical system is the part of the electric fence that converts an available power source into controlled high-voltage pulses and delivers those pulses to the fence circuit.

Understanding how the complete electrical system works is essential for professional installation, testing, maintenance and fault diagnosis.

Main System Elements

  • Power supply
  • Electric fence energizer
  • High-voltage output circuit
  • Fence conductors
  • Earth system
  • High-voltage connecting cables
  • Isolation equipment
  • Monitoring and alarm interfaces

The Basic Electrical Concept

An electric fence energizer periodically releases a controlled electrical pulse into the fence circuit.

Under normal conditions, the fence conductor remains electrically isolated from the earth and surrounding structures.

When the intended electrical path is affected, the system responds according to the design of the energizer and monitoring equipment.

Think In Systems

A professional technician does not look at the energizer, fence and earth system as separate items. They must be understood as one complete electrical system.

Safety Boundary

Electric fence energizers and their associated circuits can produce hazardous electrical pulses. Electrical installation, high-voltage connection work and testing must be performed by appropriately trained, qualified or authorised personnel in accordance with applicable requirements and manufacturer instructions.

02

The Energizer

The energizer is the central electrical component of an electric fence system. Its job is to generate controlled pulses and deliver them to the fence.

The energizer also forms an important part of the system's safety and monitoring architecture.

What The Energizer Does

  • Receives energy from its power source
  • Stores and releases electrical energy
  • Produces controlled fence pulses
  • Transfers the pulse to the fence
  • Works with the earth system
  • Monitors relevant system conditions
  • Provides status or fault indications on compatible equipment

Energizer Ratings

Energizers are manufactured with different electrical characteristics and ratings. These ratings must be understood before an energizer is selected for an installation.

The technician should refer to the manufacturer's technical documentation for the actual specifications of the selected unit.

Output And Fence Load

The electrical performance of an energizer must be considered together with the fence circuit.

Fence length, conductor arrangement, vegetation, insulation condition, leakage paths and other factors can influence the electrical load presented to the system.

Never Judge An Energizer By Size Alone

A physically larger energizer is not automatically the correct choice for a particular installation. Selection must be based on the manufacturer's rated performance and the requirements of the complete fence system.

03

Energizer Types

Electric fence energizers are available in different configurations. The correct type depends on the application, available power, environmental conditions and system design.

Common Energizer Categories

  • Mains-powered energizers
  • Battery-powered energizers
  • Solar-powered systems
  • Systems with backup power
  • Integrated security energizers

Mains-Powered Energizers

Mains-powered units are commonly used at properties where a suitable electrical supply is available.

The incoming electrical supply and associated electrical installation must meet the applicable requirements. Work on fixed mains electrical wiring must only be performed by appropriately qualified persons where required.

Battery-Powered Systems

Battery-powered energizers can be useful where mains power is unavailable or where a battery forms part of the backup strategy.

Battery condition, charging arrangements, expected operating time and environmental conditions must all be considered.

Solar-Powered Systems

Solar systems combine an energizer with a solar charging system and battery storage.

Proper system sizing is important because the available solar energy varies with location, season, weather and installation conditions.

Professional Selection

Select the energizer based on the complete system design — not simply on the length of the fence or the purchase price of the equipment.
