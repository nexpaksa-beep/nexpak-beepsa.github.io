01

The Electrical System

The electrical system is the part of the electric fence that converts an available power source into controlled high-voltage pulses and delivers those pulses to the fence circuit.

Understanding how the complete electrical system works is essential for professional installation, testing, maintenance and fault diagnosis.

Main System Elements

  • Power supply
  • Electric fence energizer
  • High-voltage output circuit
  • Fence conductors
  • Earth system
  • High-voltage connecting cables
  • Isolation equipment
  • Monitoring and alarm interfaces

The Basic Electrical Concept

An electric fence energizer periodically releases a controlled electrical pulse into the fence circuit.

Under normal conditions, the fence conductor remains electrically isolated from the earth and surrounding structures.

When the intended electrical path is affected, the system responds according to the design of the energizer and monitoring equipment.

Think In Systems

A professional technician does not look at the energizer, fence and earth system as separate items. They must be understood as one complete electrical system.

Safety Boundary

Electric fence energizers and their associated circuits can produce hazardous electrical pulses. Electrical installation, high-voltage connection work and testing must be performed by appropriately trained, qualified or authorised personnel in accordance with applicable requirements and manufacturer instructions.

02

The Energizer

The energizer is the central electrical component of an electric fence system. Its job is to generate controlled pulses and deliver them to the fence.

The energizer also forms an important part of the system's safety and monitoring architecture.

What The Energizer Does

  • Receives energy from its power source
  • Stores and releases electrical energy
  • Produces controlled fence pulses
  • Transfers the pulse to the fence
  • Works with the earth system
  • Monitors relevant system conditions
  • Provides status or fault indications on compatible equipment

Energizer Ratings

Energizers are manufactured with different electrical characteristics and ratings. These ratings must be understood before an energizer is selected for an installation.

The technician should refer to the manufacturer's technical documentation for the actual specifications of the selected unit.

Output And Fence Load

The electrical performance of an energizer must be considered together with the fence circuit.

Fence length, conductor arrangement, vegetation, insulation condition, leakage paths and other factors can influence the electrical load presented to the system.

Never Judge An Energizer By Size Alone

A physically larger energizer is not automatically the correct choice for a particular installation. Selection must be based on the manufacturer's rated performance and the requirements of the complete fence system.

03

Energizer Types

Electric fence energizers are available in different configurations. The correct type depends on the application, available power, environmental conditions and system design.

Common Energizer Categories

  • Mains-powered energizers
  • Battery-powered energizers
  • Solar-powered systems
  • Systems with backup power
  • Integrated security energizers

Mains-Powered Energizers

Mains-powered units are commonly used at properties where a suitable electrical supply is available.

The incoming electrical supply and associated electrical installation must meet the applicable requirements. Work on fixed mains electrical wiring must only be performed by appropriately qualified persons where required.

Battery-Powered Systems

Battery-powered energizers can be useful where mains power is unavailable or where a battery forms part of the backup strategy.

Battery condition, charging arrangements, expected operating time and environmental conditions must all be considered.

Solar-Powered Systems

Solar systems combine an energizer with a solar charging system and battery storage.

Proper system sizing is important because the available solar energy varies with location, season, weather and installation conditions.

Professional Selection

Select the energizer based on the complete system design — not simply on the length of the fence or the purchase price of the equipment.

04

Selecting The Correct Energizer

Selecting an energizer is a design decision, not simply a purchasing decision. The unit must be appropriate for the fence system, environmental conditions and intended security application.

A professional installer considers the complete installation before selecting the energizer.

Selection Factors

  • Total fence configuration
  • Fence length and layout
  • Number of fence sections
  • Conductor arrangement
  • Expected environmental exposure
  • Vegetation conditions
  • Required monitoring features
  • Available power source
  • Backup power requirements
  • Manufacturer specifications

Fence Length Is Not The Only Factor

Two installations with the same perimeter length may place very different demands on an energizer.

The number of conductors, insulation condition, vegetation, leakage paths and overall fence configuration can all influence system performance.

Security Application

Residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural and high-security installations may have different requirements.

The selected energizer should therefore be matched to the security design and the manufacturer's intended application.

Never Oversize Or Undersize Without A Design

An installer should not select equipment simply because it has the highest available output. Use the manufacturer's specifications and the applicable installation requirements to determine suitability.

05

High-Voltage Circuit

The high-voltage circuit carries the electrical pulses from the energizer through the fence conductors and back through the appropriate system path.

Every part of this circuit must be considered during design, installation and testing.

High-Voltage Circuit Components

  • Energizer output
  • High-voltage connecting cable
  • Fence conductors
  • Insulators
  • Connection points
  • Isolation components
  • Earth system

Electrical Continuity

The fence circuit must provide a reliable electrical path throughout the designed perimeter.

Unwanted breaks, poor connections, damaged components or unintended contact with earth can affect the performance of the system.

Electrical Leakage

Leakage occurs when electrical energy is lost through an unintended path.

Common contributors can include vegetation, damaged insulation, contaminated insulators, unsuitable connections and contact between conductive components.

Leakage Is A Fault-Finding Clue

When an electric fence is underperforming, do not immediately assume that the energizer has failed. The fence circuit, insulation, earth system and surrounding environment must all be considered.

06

High-Voltage Connections

Connections between the energizer, high-voltage cable and fence conductors are critical parts of the installation.

A poor connection can create an unreliable system even when the individual components themselves are suitable.

Professional Connection Principles

  • Use approved components
  • Follow the manufacturer's connection method
  • Maintain appropriate insulation
  • Protect connections from environmental exposure where required
  • Prevent accidental contact with conductive structures
  • Keep connections mechanically secure
  • Inspect connections during maintenance

Connection Quality

Connections should be mechanically secure and electrically reliable. They should also be suitable for the environmental conditions in which they are installed.

Outdoor installations are exposed to moisture, temperature changes, dust, UV exposure and corrosion. Component selection must account for these conditions.

Cable Entry And Routing

Where high-voltage cable enters equipment enclosures or passes through structures, the cable route should be planned to minimise mechanical damage and maintain the required separation from other services.

Never Make Improvised High-Voltage Joints

Do not use makeshift connection methods, unsuitable materials or improvised insulation on a high-voltage electric fence circuit. Use components and methods approved for the specific system.

07

Isolation & Electrical Safety

Safe isolation is one of the most important procedures when working on an electric fence.

Before maintenance, repair or modification, the system must be made safe using the appropriate isolation procedure for the equipment and installation.

Safety Procedure

  • Identify the system to be worked on
  • Isolate the appropriate power source
  • Follow the energizer manufacturer's isolation procedure
  • Prevent accidental re-energisation
  • Verify the system is safe before work begins
  • Use appropriate personal protective equipment and test equipment

Do Not Assume The Fence Is Safe

Switching off an energizer does not mean that a technician should immediately touch every part of the installation.

The correct isolation and verification procedure must be followed according to the equipment and applicable safety requirements.

Unauthorised Access

Energizer locations and electrical equipment should be protected against unauthorised access wherever required by the system design.

High-Voltage Work

Never perform high-voltage electrical work beyond your training, authorisation or qualification. When in doubt, stop work and refer the installation to a suitably qualified professional.
