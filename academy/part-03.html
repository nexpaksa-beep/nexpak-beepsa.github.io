01

Site Assessment

Before any electric fence installation begins, the site must be assessed carefully. A professional installation starts with understanding the property, its perimeter, access points, existing structures, environmental conditions and security requirements.

The installer should walk the entire perimeter and identify areas that may affect the design or installation of the electric fence.

What To Assess

  • Total perimeter length
  • Existing walls, fences and structures
  • Gates and vehicle entrances
  • Pedestrian access points
  • Slopes and changes in ground level
  • Trees and vegetation
  • Possible cable routes
  • Existing electrical infrastructure
  • Areas vulnerable to tampering
  • Suitable locations for energizers and control equipment

Understanding The Property

Every property is different. A residential property, industrial facility, farm, warehouse or commercial premises may require a different fence configuration.

The installer should therefore avoid treating every installation as a standard layout. The system must be designed around the physical characteristics and security requirements of the site.

02

Property Survey

A property survey provides the information needed to develop an accurate fence layout and installation plan.

Measurements should be recorded before materials are ordered. Incorrect measurements can result in insufficient conductors, brackets, cable or other installation components.

Survey Information

  • Perimeter measurements
  • Number and position of gates
  • Fence height
  • Number of electric fence strands
  • Corners and direction changes
  • Fence sections
  • Existing power points
  • Possible energizer locations
  • Earth system locations

Measuring The Perimeter

Measure each section separately rather than relying on an estimated total distance. Record the length of every straight section, corner and gate area.

The measurements can then be combined to determine the approximate total installation requirement.

Professional Practice

Never rely solely on visual estimation when preparing a quotation or installation plan. Accurate measurements reduce material shortages, unnecessary waste and installation problems.

03

Perimeter Planning

Once the site has been surveyed, the perimeter can be divided into manageable fence sections.

Perimeter planning determines how the fence will physically follow the property while maintaining appropriate protection and allowing for gates, corners and other structures.

Planning Considerations

  • Fence direction
  • Corners
  • Changes in elevation
  • Gate locations
  • Wall and fence conditions
  • Vegetation
  • Access requirements
  • Security zones

Dividing The Fence Into Sections

Large properties should be divided logically into sections. This makes the installation easier to understand, maintain and troubleshoot.

Section planning also helps determine where monitoring equipment, warning signs, gates and other security components need to be incorporated.

04

Fence Layout

The fence layout converts the site survey into a practical installation design.

The layout should show where the electric fence follows the perimeter and where changes occur around corners, gates, buildings and other structures.

Layout Should Identify

  • Fence line
  • Electric fence sections
  • Gates
  • Corners
  • Energizer location
  • Earth system
  • High-voltage cable routes
  • Monitoring equipment

A clear layout allows the installer to understand the complete system before physical installation begins.
