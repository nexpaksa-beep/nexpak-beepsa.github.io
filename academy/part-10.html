01

Maintenance Fundamentals

An electric-fence system is an outdoor security installation and should not be treated as maintenance-free equipment.

Regular inspection and maintenance help identify deterioration before it develops into a major security or reliability problem.

Maintenance Objectives

  • Maintain security performance
  • Identify deterioration
  • Reduce avoidable faults
  • Maintain structural integrity
  • Maintain electrical performance
  • Identify safety concerns
  • Extend equipment service life
  • Maintain accurate system records

Preventive Maintenance

Preventive maintenance aims to identify and correct developing problems before they result in system failure.

Examples include inspecting vegetation, checking mechanical supports, examining connections and monitoring system performance.

Reactive Maintenance

Reactive maintenance occurs after a fault or failure has already developed.

Although faults cannot always be prevented, effective preventive maintenance can reduce many avoidable failures.

Professional Principle

The best maintenance problem is the one identified before it becomes a security incident.

02

Routine Inspection

Routine inspection provides an opportunity to identify physical and electrical conditions that could affect system performance.

Inspection Areas

  • Fence alignment
  • Posts and brackets
  • Conductors
  • Insulators
  • Connections
  • Vegetation
  • Gates
  • High-voltage cable routes
  • Energizer condition
  • Earth system
  • Warning signs
  • Alarm and monitoring equipment

Visual Inspection

Begin with a visual inspection before performing detailed electrical testing.

Look for broken components, physical impact, corrosion, vegetation contact, damaged insulation and other obvious problems.

Walk The Entire Perimeter

A professional inspection should not stop at the energizer or the first section of fence.

The complete protected perimeter should be considered according to the scope of the inspection.

Do Not Inspect Only Where The Customer Complained

A reported fault may be a symptom of a larger maintenance problem elsewhere in the system. Inspect the relevant system as a whole.

03

Vegetation Management

Vegetation is one of the most common environmental factors affecting outdoor electric-fence systems.

Plants can grow into the fence, contact conductors and create leakage conditions.

Vegetation Risks

  • Electrical leakage
  • Reduced fence performance
  • Increased nuisance alarms
  • Mechanical pressure on conductors
  • Damage to insulators
  • Increased maintenance requirements

Regular Clearance

Vegetation around the fence should be managed according to the design, environmental conditions and applicable requirements.

Fast-Growing Areas

Some properties may require more frequent inspection because vegetation growth can change rapidly.

Do Not Ignore Seasonal Growth

A fence that performs correctly during a dry season may develop leakage problems when vegetation becomes dense or wet.

04

Conductor Maintenance

Conductors are exposed to weather, mechanical forces, vegetation and accidental impact.

Their condition should be checked regularly as part of perimeter maintenance.

Inspect For

  • Breaks
  • Excessive sag
  • Unusual tension
  • Corrosion
  • Damaged joins
  • Contact with structures
  • Physical deformation

Mechanical Condition

A conductor should remain properly supported and aligned throughout its intended route.

Electrical Continuity

Where a conductor or connection is suspected of causing poor performance, appropriate testing should be used to determine the condition.

Replacement

Damaged conductors should be repaired or replaced using suitable materials and methods appropriate for the fence system.

Maintenance Principle

Do not wait for a conductor to fail completely before investigating visible deterioration.

05

Insulator Maintenance

Insulators keep the electric-fence conductors electrically separated from supporting structures. Their condition is therefore critical to system performance.

Inspect Insulators For

  • Cracks
  • Breakage
  • Contamination
  • Physical movement
  • UV deterioration
  • Signs of electrical tracking
  • Incorrect positioning

Environmental Exposure

Outdoor insulators are exposed to sunlight, rain, dust, pollution and temperature changes. Over time these conditions can affect the condition of materials.

Contamination

Dirt and other contamination may create unwanted leakage paths, particularly when combined with moisture.

Damaged Insulators

A visibly damaged insulator should be assessed and replaced where necessary using a suitable component for the specific installation.

Never Ignore Small Cracks

A component that appears only slightly damaged may deteriorate further under continued outdoor exposure and mechanical loading.

06

Connections & Corrosion

Electrical connections are critical points in an electric-fence system. Poor connections can increase resistance, create unreliable operation or eventually result in an open circuit.

Common Connection Problems

  • Loose connections
  • Corrosion
  • Poor mechanical joints
  • Inappropriate joining methods
  • Environmental contamination
  • Mechanical movement
  • Damaged connection hardware

Corrosion

Corrosion can develop when conductive materials are exposed to moisture, chemicals and environmental contaminants.

Corroded connections should be assessed rather than simply covered up.

Connection Inspection

Look for discoloration, oxidation, loose hardware, damaged conductors and other visible signs of deterioration.

Repair Quality

Repairs should use appropriate components and joining methods suitable for electric-fence applications.

Do Not Hide A Bad Connection

Covering a deteriorated connection without correcting the underlying problem can allow the fault to return.

Maintenance Principle

Every connection should be mechanically secure, electrically appropriate and protected against the conditions for which it was designed.

07

Earth System Maintenance

The earth system is a fundamental part of electric-fence operation and should be included in routine maintenance.

Earth System Inspection

  • Inspect accessible connections
  • Check for corrosion
  • Check for physical damage
  • Inspect conductor condition
  • Consider changes in the surrounding environment
  • Perform appropriate testing when required

Earth Connections

Earth connections should remain secure and in good condition. Deteriorated connections should be investigated and corrected appropriately.

Environmental Changes

Construction, landscaping, soil disturbance and other property changes may affect an existing earth system.

Periodic Testing

Where required by the system design, manufacturer's instructions or applicable standards, appropriate earth testing should be included in the maintenance programme.

Earth Maintenance Is Not Optional

A fence can appear physically perfect while still having an earth-system problem. Include the earth system in the diagnostic process.

08

Energizer Servicing

The energizer is the primary source of the fence's electrical pulses and should receive appropriate inspection and servicing.

Energizer Service Checks

  • Physical condition
  • Power supply condition
  • Indicator operation
  • Output performance
  • Connection condition
  • Enclosure condition
  • Environmental exposure
  • Backup power equipment where installed

Enclosure Condition

The energizer enclosure should be inspected for physical damage, moisture ingress and other conditions that could affect the equipment.

Power Supply

Confirm that the energizer is receiving the required supply and that the supply equipment remains in suitable condition.

Output Performance

Appropriate electric-fence test equipment should be used to assess output performance according to the manufacturer's specifications and the requirements applicable to the installation.

Manufacturer Instructions

Servicing procedures vary between energizer models. Technicians must follow the relevant manufacturer's instructions rather than applying assumptions from another product.

Do Not Open Equipment Without Authorisation

Internal servicing of energizers should only be performed by appropriately qualified or authorised personnel following the manufacturer's procedures.

Professional Principle

Maintain the energizer as part of the complete security system — not as an isolated piece of equipment.
