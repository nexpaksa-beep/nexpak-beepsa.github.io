Part 16 — System Maintenance & Preventative Servicing

A professional security system should not only be installed correctly. It must also be maintained throughout its service life.

Preventative maintenance helps identify developing problems before they become system failures.

Regular servicing can improve reliability, extend equipment life and help ensure that the security system continues to perform as intended.

Why Maintenance Matters

Security equipment operates continuously and is often exposed to electrical, mechanical and environmental conditions that can affect performance over time.

Dust, moisture, corrosion, temperature changes, vibration, damaged cables and ageing components can gradually reduce system reliability.

A maintenance programme allows these conditions to be identified and addressed before they develop into serious faults.

Preventative Maintenance

Preventative maintenance is planned work performed to reduce the likelihood of equipment failure.

It is different from reactive maintenance, which takes place after a component or system has already failed.

A professional technician should use both inspection and testing to determine the condition of the security installation.

Maintenance Planning

Maintenance should be planned according to the type of equipment, installation environment, manufacturer requirements and operational importance of the system.

High-risk or critical installations may require more frequent inspections than smaller systems.

Maintenance Inspection

A maintenance inspection begins with a careful visual examination of the complete security installation.

The technician should look for signs of physical damage, corrosion, loose fittings, damaged cables, water ingress and other conditions that may affect system operation.

Equipment Condition

Inspect control panels, detectors, cameras, power supplies, batteries, access-control equipment and other installed devices.

Equipment should be clean, securely mounted and free from obvious damage.

Check Connections

Electrical and communication connections should be inspected for looseness, corrosion or other signs of deterioration.

Connections that show signs of damage should be investigated and repaired using the correct procedures and suitable components.

Inspect Cable Routes

Cable routes should be checked for physical damage, exposure to environmental conditions and signs of interference.

Outdoor cables require particular attention because sunlight, moisture, temperature changes and mechanical activity can affect their condition over time.

Clean Equipment

Dust and dirt can affect the performance of some security equipment.

Cameras, sensors, control equipment and ventilation openings should be cleaned using methods appropriate for the equipment.

Cleaning should never damage sensitive components or compromise equipment protection.

Battery Maintenance

Backup batteries are critical components in many security systems because they allow equipment to continue operating when the primary power supply is interrupted.

Batteries should be inspected regularly for signs of ageing, damage, leakage, corrosion or poor connections.

Battery Connections

Check that battery terminals and connecting leads are secure and free from excessive corrosion.

Loose or corroded connections can increase resistance and prevent the battery from supplying the required power.

Battery Charging

Where applicable, confirm that the charging system is operating according to the equipment manufacturer's requirements.

A battery that is repeatedly undercharged may have a significantly reduced service life.

Battery Replacement

Batteries are consumable components and eventually require replacement.

Replacement batteries must be suitable for the specific equipment and installed using the manufacturer's recommended procedure.

Do not install an unsuitable battery simply because its physical size appears to fit.

CCTV Maintenance

CCTV systems require regular inspection to maintain image quality and reliable recording.

Check camera lenses, housings, mounting brackets, cables, power supplies and recording equipment.

Camera Image Quality

Inspect images for dirt, obstruction, excessive glare, poor focus or other conditions that may reduce useful visibility.

CCTV Recording

Confirm that cameras are recording correctly and that recorded footage can be retrieved when required.

Check available storage capacity and verify that the recording configuration remains appropriate for the customer's requirements.

Camera Housing

Outdoor camera housings should be inspected for cracks, damage, moisture or other signs that the protective enclosure may no longer be providing adequate protection.

Any environmental damage should be addressed before it causes further deterioration of the camera or associated electronics.

Alarm System Maintenance

Alarm systems should be inspected and tested regularly to ensure that detection devices, control equipment and notification functions continue to operate correctly.

Detection Devices

Inspect detectors for physical damage, contamination, obstruction and changes in their surrounding environment.

Detection devices should be tested according to the manufacturer's instructions and the maintenance procedure for the installation.

Alarm Control Panel

Inspect the alarm control panel for fault indications, power problems, battery warnings and other system notifications.

Any persistent fault indication should be investigated rather than simply cleared.

Alarm Notifications

Where notification services are installed, confirm that the appropriate communication path and notification functions are operating correctly.

Testing should be coordinated appropriately so that monitoring personnel or authorised users are not unnecessarily dispatched.
