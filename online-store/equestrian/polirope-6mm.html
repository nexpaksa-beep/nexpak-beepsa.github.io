What Is 6mm Poly Rope?

6mm poly rope, also known as polirope, is a flexible electric-fence conductor commonly used for equestrian fencing.

Unlike wide poly tape, the round rope profile allows air to pass through the conductor more easily. This can make it particularly useful in exposed areas where wind loading on wide tape may be a concern.

Poly rope contains conductive strands woven into the rope structure. These conductors carry the electrical pulse supplied by the energiser around the fence.

Training Point Think of 6mm poly rope as a flexible electrical conductor that also provides a visible physical boundary for horses.

Key Performance Factors

Electrical Conductivity Lower resistance allows the electrical pulse to travel further with less voltage loss along the fence.
Mechanical Strength A stronger rope is better suited to applications where the fence is exposed to physical pressure or tension.
Wind Performance The narrow round profile allows wind to pass through more easily than wide poly tape.
Visibility High-visibility colours help horses recognise the fence boundary.

Voltage Loss Over Distance

Electrical resistance becomes increasingly important as the fence becomes longer. Higher-resistance conductors experience greater voltage loss over distance.

JVA's standard stainless-steel construction is suited to shorter applications, while low-resistance Nemtek rope options are designed to maintain stronger electrical performance over longer runs.

Correct Installation

The exact installation method depends on the rope, insulators, posts and energiser being used. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for the specific products.

1

Plan the Fence Line

Determine the required fence route, gate positions, corners and the number of rope strands before installation begins.

2

Install the Posts

Position the permanent or temporary posts at suitable intervals and make sure the posts are secure and aligned.

3

Fit the Insulators

Install suitable rope insulators on the posts. The rope should remain electrically isolated from the post.

4

Run the Poly Rope

Thread the 6mm rope through the compatible insulators and maintain consistent tension without overstretching the conductor.

5

Make Proper Joins

Where two lengths of rope meet, use a suitable electric-fence connector rather than tying a conventional knot.

6

Connect the Energiser

Connect the fence system using suitable high-voltage cable and compatible electrical connections.

7

Test the Fence

After installation, use a suitable electric fence tester to confirm that the fence is receiving the required voltage.

⚠️

Common Installation Mistakes

Small installation errors can affect both the mechanical performance and electrical effectiveness of an electric horse fence.

!
Tying Knots Conventional knots can damage the conductor strands and create a poor electrical connection.
!
Incorrect Insulators Using an unsuitable insulator can allow the rope to move, rub or make unwanted contact with the post.
!
Excessive Tension Over-tensioning can place unnecessary stress on the rope, posts and connections.
!
Poor Electrical Joins Loose or unsuitable connectors can increase resistance and reduce electrical performance.
!
Vegetation Contact Grass, branches and other vegetation touching the fence can cause leakage and reduce voltage.
!
Not Testing the Fence A fence should be checked with a suitable tester after installation and during routine maintenance.

Safety & Best Practice

Electric horse fencing must be designed and installed responsibly. The fence should provide a clearly visible boundary while being suitable for the animals and property.

  • Switch off and isolate the energiser before working on the fence.
  • Use only components compatible with the rope, energiser and fencing system.
  • Inspect rope, insulators and connections regularly for wear or damage.
  • Keep vegetation away from the electrified conductor.
  • Ensure gates and access points are designed so horses cannot become entangled.
  • Use a suitable electric fence tester to check system voltage.
  • Follow the manufacturer's installation and safety instructions.
🔍

Inspection & Maintenance

Regular inspection helps maintain both the electrical performance and physical condition of a 6mm poly rope fence.

Inspect the Rope Check for fraying, cuts, stretching, broken conductor strands or other visible damage.
Check Insulators Make sure insulators remain secure, clean and properly isolate the rope from the posts.
Check Connections Inspect joiners, clamps and energiser connections for looseness, corrosion or damage.
Measure Fence Voltage Use a suitable fence tester to identify voltage loss and confirm that the system is operating correctly.
