About 40mm Horse Fence Tape

40mm electric horse fence tape, commonly referred to as polytape, is designed to provide a highly visible electric barrier for equestrian fencing applications.

The wide tape construction provides substantially more visual presence than narrow electric fence wire, making it particularly suitable for horse paddocks, arenas, temporary grazing areas and boundary fencing.

Important: Horse fencing is not simply about choosing a wide tape. The complete system should be properly designed using compatible tape, insulators, connectors, posts, earthing and an appropriate energiser.
📋

How the Tape Works

Electric fence tape combines a flexible polymer tape structure with conductive metal strands. When the tape is correctly connected to an energised electric fencing system, an electrical pulse travels through the conductive elements.

The tape therefore performs two important functions: it provides a highly visible physical boundary and it carries the electrical pulse throughout the fence circuit.

Visible Boundary

  • 40mm width provides strong visibility.
  • Suitable for horse paddocks and equestrian boundaries.
  • Helps make the fence visually obvious to animals and people.

Electrical Conductor

  • Integrated metal conductors carry the electrical pulse.
  • Connections must maintain good electrical contact.
  • Damaged or poorly connected tape can reduce system performance.
📊

Standard vs Low-Resistance Tape

Not all 40mm tape is electrically identical. Different products can use different numbers and types of conductive strands.

Specification Standard Poly Tape Heavy-Duty / Low Resistance
Conductors Commonly 6–10 stainless-steel strands May use stainless-steel and tinned copper conductors
Typical Tensile Strength Approximately 120–190kg, depending on product Some heavy-duty products can reach significantly higher tensile strengths
Electrical Resistance Higher resistance; product specification must be checked Much lower resistance on suitable long-distance products
Typical Application Shorter or portable fences Longer or more demanding permanent installations
Selection rule: Do not choose tape based only on width. For longer fences, check the manufacturer's resistance specification and select an appropriate low-resistance product where required.

Correct Installation

Correct installation is essential for both the effectiveness of the fence and the safety of the animals.

1

Plan the Fence

Determine the fence route, total length, number of corners, gates, posts and the required energiser capacity before installation.

2

Use Suitable Supports

Install suitable posts and horse-fence insulators designed to support and electrically isolate the tape.

3

Install the Tape

Feed the 40mm tape through compatible insulators and keep the tape properly aligned throughout the fence.

4

Maintain Appropriate Tension

Tension the tape sufficiently to keep the fence stable without placing unnecessary mechanical stress on the tape or fittings.

5

Make Proper Connections

Use compatible tape buckles, connectors and gate hardware to maintain reliable electrical continuity.

6

Test the Completed Fence

Switch on the energiser and use an appropriate electric fence tester to confirm that the system is operating correctly.

✅

Installation Best Practice

Keep Tape Visible Avoid installing the tape where vegetation or structures hide it.
Use Compatible Components Buckles, insulators and connectors should be suitable for 40mm tape.
Avoid Sharp Edges Ensure the tape does not rub against sharp or abrasive surfaces.
Protect Connections Connections should remain mechanically secure and protected from unnecessary movement.
Use Correct Earthing The energiser and earth system must be installed according to manufacturer requirements.
Test Regularly Periodically check fence voltage with a suitable tester.

Common Installation Mistakes

Many electric fence problems are caused not by the tape itself, but by incorrect installation, poor connections or inadequate maintenance.

!
Using the Wrong Tape for the Distance Higher-resistance tape may not be appropriate for long fence runs.
!
Poor Electrical Connections Loose or unsuitable connections can increase resistance and reduce electrical performance.
!
Over-Tensioning Excessive tension can damage tape, fittings or supporting structures.
!
Damaged Tape Frayed, cut or heavily damaged tape should be repaired or replaced.
!
Vegetation Contact Vegetation touching the electric conductor can reduce fence voltage.
!
Not Testing the Fence A fence should be checked using an appropriate fence tester rather than relying on guesswork.

Safety & Responsible Use

Electric fencing must be installed and operated responsibly. Horse fencing requires particular attention to animal behaviour, visibility and appropriate system design.

  • Switch off and isolate the energiser before working on the fence.
  • Use compatible fencing components.
  • Follow the energiser manufacturer's installation requirements.
  • Inspect the fence regularly for damage.
  • Keep vegetation from interfering with the electrical circuit.
  • Use a suitable electric fence tester.
  • Ensure the fence is clearly visible and appropriately designed for horses.
  • Never assume that a fence is de-energised without following the correct isolation procedure.
🎓

What You Should Know

Before selecting 40mm horse fence tape, consider the total fence length, whether the installation is permanent or temporary, the required visibility, the expected mechanical loading and the electrical resistance of the selected tape.

The key principle: A good horse fencing installation is a complete system. The tape, connectors, insulators, energiser, earth system, posts and gate arrangements all need to work together.
