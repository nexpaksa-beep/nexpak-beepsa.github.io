Horse Fence Corner Strain Insulators

Understanding the correct use of corner and end strain insulators is essential for building a secure, properly tensioned and reliable electric horse-fencing system.

🐎

What Is a Corner Strain Insulator?

A corner strain insulator is a critical component used at the ends, corners and changes of direction of an electric horse fence.

Its primary purpose is to isolate the electrified tape from the supporting post while allowing the tape to be securely tensioned.

In an equestrian application, the component must also be strong enough to handle fence tension while avoiding unnecessary sharp or exposed edges that could create a hazard for horses.

📍

Where Are Corner Strain Insulators Used?

01

End Posts

Used where a fence line terminates and the tape needs to be securely anchored and tensioned.

02

90° Corners

Used where the fence changes direction and the tape must remain properly tensioned.

03

Gate Posts

Can be used with suitable activator plates and gate-handle arrangements at paddock entrances.

04

Multiple Directions

Multi-directional systems can allow several fence runs or gate connections to originate from one suitable post.

05

Tension Points

Provides a secure point from which compatible horse tape can be tensioned without transferring the electrical connection directly into the post.

06

Paddock Boundaries

Forms part of properly constructed equestrian boundary systems where strong, visible and reliable tape support is required.

Correct Installation

Correct installation is important because the corner point carries significant mechanical tension from the fence line.

1

Position the Strainer Post

Install a strong, properly braced end or corner post capable of end or corner post capable of handling the expected fence tension.

2

Mount the Insulator

Secure the appropriate insulator firmly to the post using the manufacturer's recommended fixing method.

3

Insert the Tape

Position the compatible horse tape through the insulator or connection point according to the product design.

4

Apply Correct Tension

Tension the tape evenly without overstressing the tape, buckle, insulator or supporting post.

5

Inspect the Connection

Check that the tape is secure, properly aligned and free from excessive rubbing or sharp contact.

6

Test the Fence

After installation, use a suitable electric fence tester to confirm that the system is operating correctly.

Example: 100 Metre Fence Layout

For training purposes, a 100 metre fence can be planned using approximately 25 intermediate posts when using a nominal 4 metre spacing.

Intermediate Posts Approximately 25 posts at 4 metre spacing.
End Points Suitable end strainers are required to carry the fence tension.
Corner Points Corner posts require appropriate bracing and strain insulation.
Tape Lines Each horizontal tape line requires its own appropriate support system.
Important: Actual post spacing and bracing requirements depend on terrain, fence design, tape type, tension and the manufacturer's installation recommendations.

Common Installation Mistakes

!
Weak Corner Posts Using an inadequately braced post can cause movement when the tape is tensioned.
!
Incorrect Insulator Using a component that is not designed for the tape profile can cause poor support.
!
Excessive Tension Over-tensioning can damage the tape, buckle, insulator or post.
!
Poor Alignment Misaligned tape can rub against posts and fittings, accelerating wear.
!
Exposed Sharp Hardware Hardware should be installed in a way that minimises potential injury risks to horses.
!
No Fence Testing Always verify the completed system with a suitable electric fence tester.
🔍

Inspection & Maintenance

Corner and end strain points should be inspected regularly because they carry much of the mechanical tension in the fence.

Check the Post Look for movement, leaning or signs that the post is losing stability.
Check the Insulator Inspect for cracks, UV deterioration, loose fasteners or damage.
Inspect the Tape Look for fraying, cuts, stretching and excessive rubbing.
Test the Voltage Use a suitable fence tester to confirm that the fence remains electrically effective.

Safety & Best Practice

Electric horse fencing must be designed and maintained with both electrical safety and animal safety in mind.

  • Switch off and isolate the energiser before working on the fence.
  • Use only components compatible with the fencing tape and system.
  • Ensure corner and end posts are properly braced for the expected tension.
  • Avoid exposed sharp edges or unnecessary protruding hardware.
  • Inspect the fence regularly for damage and deterioration.
  • Use a suitable fence tester to verify electrical performance.
  • Follow the manufacturer's instructions for all fencing components.
