UNDERSTANDING HARD HT CABLE

What Is Hard HT Cable?

Hard HT (High Tension) cable is a double-insulated electrical fence cable containing a solid metal conductor. It is designed for applications where high-voltage fence current needs to be carried safely over a distance.

Unlike flexible soft HT cable, which uses multiple fine conductor strands, Hard HT cable uses a solid-core conductor. This gives the cable greater mechanical rigidity and makes it particularly suitable for underground routes, lead-outs and under-gate installations.

🎓 TRAINING NOTE

Think of Hard HT cable as the electrical highway between two parts of an electric fence system. The cable must provide a reliable electrical path while remaining properly insulated from the surrounding ground, posts, metalwork and other conductive materials.

Where Is Hard HT Cable Used?

Hard HT cable is commonly used in professional electric fencing installations where ordinary fence wire cannot safely or conveniently carry the connection.

Under-Gate Connections

Used to carry electrical current underneath gates and openings so that separate fence sections remain electrically connected.

Energizer Lead-Outs

Used to carry high-voltage pulses from the energizer toward the fence system where the energizer is not mounted directly beside the fence.

Paddock Connections

Useful when multiple paddocks or fence sections need to receive power from the same energizer system.

Protected Cable Routes

Double insulation helps protect the high-voltage conductor from accidental contact with surrounding materials.

INSTALLATION TRAINING

How to Install Hard HT Cable

Correct installation is just as important as selecting the correct cable.

1

Plan the Cable Route

Plan the route before digging. Keep the cable run as direct as practical and identify every gate, fence section and connection point that requires power.

2

Use Suitable Conduit Underground

Where the cable is buried, use suitable protective conduit according to the installation requirements. This provides additional protection against stones, crushing, digging and mechanical damage.

3

Avoid Sharp Kinks

Hard HT cable contains a solid conductor and should not be sharply kinked. Use gradual bends when changing direction.

4

Protect Entry and Exit Points

Where the cable leaves the ground or conduit, protect the cable against abrasion and ensure it cannot rub against sharp metal edges.

5

Make Secure Electrical Connections

Use suitable connectors, clamps or approved connection hardware for the particular fence components. Connections should be mechanically secure and electrically reliable.

6

Test the Fence

After installation, test the fence voltage and inspect the complete route for faults, poor connections, unwanted contact and voltage loss.

⚠️ Important Installation Rules

  • Do not deliberately kink or sharply crease solid-core Hard HT cable.
  • Do not allow the conductor or damaged insulation to make unwanted contact with earth or metal structures.
  • Protect underground cable from mechanical damage.
  • Use suitable high-voltage fence connectors for the electrical connection.
  • Keep connections clean, tight and properly insulated.
  • Inspect underground and under-gate cable routes during routine fence maintenance.

🎓 TRAINING TIP

When designing multiple paddocks, plan the electrical distribution before installing the physical fence. This makes it much easier to identify where under-gate cables and lead-outs are required.

Hard HT Cable Maintenance

Inspect Above-Ground Sections

Check exposed sections for cracking, cuts, abrasion, animal damage or contact with sharp edges.

Check Connections

Loose or corroded connections can cause poor fence performance and unnecessary voltage loss.

Monitor Fence Voltage

A sudden voltage drop can indicate a damaged cable, faulty connection, vegetation contact or another fence fault.

Protect During Farm Work

Mark buried cable routes where appropriate so future digging, fencing or agricultural work does not damage the installation.
