About 12mm Poly Tape

12mm poly tape is commonly used in equestrian electric fencing where a lightweight and highly visible conductor is required.

It is particularly useful for temporary horse paddocks, strip grazing and portable fencing. It can also be used as a secondary lower strand beneath a wider 40mm horse tape.

The tape consists of UV-stabilized polyethylene woven together with conductive metal strands. These conductors carry the electrical pulse from the energiser through the fence.

Why visibility matters Horses can see a wide, contrasting fence conductor more easily than a thin wire. Good visibility can help horses recognise the fence boundary and reduce accidental contact.

Electrical Performance

The resistance of electric fence tape determines how easily the electrical pulse travels along the conductor.

Stainless Steel Conductors Durable and corrosion resistant, making them suitable for outdoor fencing applications.
Mixed-Metal Conductors Some premium tapes combine stainless steel with highly conductive metals to reduce resistance.
Longer Fence Runs Low-resistance tape is generally better suited to longer installations.
Short Portable Runs Standard stainless-steel tape can be practical for shorter temporary paddocks.

How 12mm Poly Tape Works

12mm poly tape combines a UV-stabilised polyethylene tape with conductive metal strands woven into the material.

When connected to a suitable electric fence energiser, the conductive strands carry the electrical pulse along the fence line.

The visible tape also provides a clear visual boundary for horses, helping them recognise the location of the fence.

Correct Installation

Proper installation helps maintain visibility, electrical continuity and the physical integrity of the tape.

1

Plan the Fence

Determine the fence route, number of tape lines, post positions and gate locations before installation.

2

Install Suitable Posts

Position posts at appropriate spacing for the terrain and fencing system. Use suitable intermediate insulators to support the tape.

3

Install the Tape

Thread the tape through compatible insulators and maintain even tension without excessively stretching the tape.

4

Make Proper Connections

Use suitable tape joiners, buckles and connectors rather than tying knots in the tape.

5

Connect the Energiser

Connect the completed fence circuit to a suitable energiser according to the manufacturer's instructions.

6

Test the Fence

Use a suitable electric fence tester to confirm that the fence is operating correctly before introducing horses.

⚠️

Common Installation Mistakes

Small installation mistakes can reduce the performance and lifespan of electric fence tape. Avoid the following common problems.

!
Tying Knots in the Tape Knots can damage the conductive strands and create a poor electrical connection.
!
Incorrect Joiners Always use a joiner designed for the width and type of tape being installed.
!
Excessive Tension Over-tensioning can place unnecessary stress on the tape and its conductive strands.
!
Damaged Tape Torn, frayed or badly damaged tape should be repaired or replaced.
!
Poor Insulation Incorrect or damaged insulators can allow the tape to contact posts and other conductive materials.
!
Not Testing Voltage Always verify fence voltage with a suitable fence tester after installation and during maintenance.

Safety & Best Practice

Electric fencing should be installed and maintained responsibly. Horse fencing should also be designed with animal visibility and safety in mind.

  • Switch off and isolate the energiser before working on the fence.
  • Use only compatible tape, insulators, joiners and electrical components.
  • Inspect the tape regularly for cuts, fraying and damage.
  • Keep vegetation away from the conductive tape to reduce unnecessary leakage.
  • Use a suitable fence tester to check system voltage.
  • Make sure gates and access points are properly insulated and connected.
  • Follow the manufacturer's instructions for the tape and energiser being used.

Inspection & Maintenance

Regular inspection helps maintain the visibility, conductivity and mechanical condition of your horse fencing.

Inspect the Tape Look for cuts, fraying, stretching, fading or other visible damage.
Check Joiners Make sure connections remain tight, clean and electrically continuous.
Check Insulators Replace cracked or damaged insulators that may allow electrical leakage.
Test Fence Voltage Periodically test the fence with an appropriate electric fence tester.
