A professional guide to selecting, installing and correctly using insulated gate handles and gate-break systems for horse fencing.

What Are Gate Handles & Gate Breaks?

Electric fence gate handles and gate-break systems allow paddock and field entrances to be opened and closed while maintaining the electrical integrity of the surrounding fence.

For equestrian applications, the gate system should provide a secure mechanical connection, good electrical continuity and sufficient insulation so that the user can operate the gate safely.

High-visibility tape gates can also provide a clearly visible boundary for horses when correctly designed and installed.

Correct Gate Installation

The exact installation method depends on the gate handle, tape or rope and the associated insulators and connectors. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for the specific components being installed.

1

Switch Off the Energiser

Before installing, repairing or adjusting an electric fence gate, switch off and isolate the energiser according to the manufacturer's instructions.

2

Plan the Gate Opening

Determine the required opening width and ensure the selected handle, tape, rope or spring system is suitable for that span.

3

Install the Gate Hardware

Secure the appropriate activator plate, corner component or gate connector to the supporting post.

4

Attach the Conductor

Connect the compatible horse tape, rope or spring to the gate handle and connection hardware.

5

Check Insulation

Confirm that the handle and insulated components are intact and that the user cannot directly contact the live conductor during normal operation.

6

Test the Gate

Restore the fence and use a suitable electric-fence tester to confirm that voltage is present on the appropriate sections of the system.

Correct Use of a Gate Handle

Use the Insulated Grip Always operate the gate using the insulated handle rather than touching the conductive tape, wire or spring.
Close the Gate Properly Ensure the handle is correctly seated on its connection point when the gate is closed.
Avoid Dragging the Tape Do not allow the tape to drag on the ground where it can become damaged, contaminated or tangled.
Keep Horses Safe Gate arrangements should be positioned and tensioned appropriately for the horses using the enclosure.

Common Installation Mistakes

!
Using the Wrong Handle Using a handle that is not suitable for the selected tape, rope, spring or gate span.
!
Poor Electrical Contact The gate connection does not make reliable contact with the live conductor.
!
Damaged Insulation A cracked, damaged or deteriorated handle is left in service.
!
Excessive Gate Tension Excessive tension can place unnecessary mechanical stress on the gate components.
!
Tape Touching the Ground Low or sagging tape can become damaged or create unwanted electrical leakage.
!
Not Testing the Gate The completed gate is not checked with a suitable fence tester.

Safety & Best Practice

Electric fencing must be installed and maintained responsibly. Horse-fencing gates should also be designed with the behaviour, visibility and safety of the animals in mind.

  • Switch off and isolate the energiser before working on the gate.
  • Never use a damaged or cracked insulated gate handle.
  • Use components compatible with the tape, rope or spring being installed.
  • Inspect gate handles, springs, tape and connection hardware regularly.
  • Keep the gate conductor clear of the ground and unwanted vegetation.
  • Use an appropriate fence tester to verify electrical performance.
  • Follow the manufacturer's installation and safety instructions.
🔍

Inspection & Maintenance

Inspect the Handle Check the plastic body, grip and hook for cracking, damage or deterioration.
Inspect the Spring Check spring systems for corrosion, stretching or mechanical damage.
Check the Tape Look for fraying, cuts, excessive stretching or broken conductive wires.
Test the Fence Use a suitable fence tester to check that the gate and surrounding fence are operating correctly.
