Active Perimeter Protection

More Than a Fence. An Active Security Barrier.

A conventional boundary fence can define the edge of a property, but it does not necessarily prevent an intruder from climbing over it. An electric fence adds an active deterrent to the perimeter by delivering a controlled high-voltage, low-current pulse when the fence is touched or breached.

A properly designed system can also provide alarm signalling when the fence is cut, shorted or otherwise compromised, allowing the perimeter to become part of a wider integrated security strategy.

  • Strong visual and physical deterrent
  • Active perimeter monitoring
  • Available for residential, commercial and industrial properties
  • Can integrate with broader security systems

Designed to Detect. Designed to Deter.

Electric fencing works as part of a layered security approach. The objective is not simply to deliver an electric pulse, but to create a controlled perimeter that discourages intrusion and can provide an early warning when the fence is interfered with.

How The Technology Works

How Does an Electric Fence Work?

An electric fence combines an energizer, conductors, insulators, earthing and monitoring components to create an active perimeter-security barrier.

01

The Energizer

The energizer is the heart of the electric fencing system. It converts its power source into controlled high-voltage, short-duration pulses that are sent through the fence conductors.

The pulse is designed to provide a strong deterrent while limiting the amount of electrical energy delivered by each pulse.

02

Fence Conductors

Specialised electric-fence wires carry the energizer's pulses around the protected perimeter.

The conductors are supported by suitable insulators and mounted at carefully selected heights and spacing to create an effective barrier while maintaining the required electrical separation from surrounding structures.

03

The Earth System

A properly designed earthing system provides the electrical return path required for the fence circuit to operate correctly.

Poor earthing can significantly affect fence performance, which is why earth rods, connections and cable selection are important parts of a professional installation.

04

Detection & Deterrence

When an intruder makes contact with the electrified conductors, the system can deliver its controlled pulse, creating an immediate deterrent.

Depending on the system design, interference such as cutting, shorting or tampering with the fence can also trigger an alarm condition.

The Electrical Circuit

Why the Complete System Matters

An electric fence is not simply a set of wires connected to an energizer. The energizer, fence conductors, insulators, earth system, connectors and monitoring equipment all need to work together.

A fault in one part of the installation can affect the performance of the entire perimeter. Vegetation touching the conductors, damaged insulators, poor connections, inadequate earthing or incorrect installation can result in voltage loss and reduced system effectiveness.

For this reason, electric fencing should be approached as a complete security system rather than as an individual piece of equipment.

Professional system design matters

The correct energizer capacity, fence configuration, earthing arrangement, conductor layout and monitoring equipment depend on the property and installation.

Before You Buy

Don't Choose an Energizer by Size Alone

A common mistake is choosing an electric-fence energizer simply because it has a higher advertised output. The correct choice depends on the complete installation, including the perimeter length, fence design, vegetation, environmental conditions and required security configuration.

The supporting components matter just as much. Poor-quality connections, damaged insulators, inadequate earthing or incorrect mounting can reduce the effectiveness of an otherwise capable energizer.

The first consideration is the type of property being protected. A residential home, business premises, warehouse, farm or industrial site may require very different perimeter configurations.

  • Commercial premises
  • Industrial facilities
  • Farms and larger properties

Consider the Perimeter

The total perimeter length affects energizer selection, fence configuration and installation requirements. Long or complex perimeters may also benefit from being divided into separate monitored zones.

  • Number of corners
  • Gates and access points

Look at the Existing Boundary

  • Integrated security

Plan for Power Continuity

Installation Planning

What Should Be Considered During Installation?

A reliable electric fence depends on more than the energizer. The physical perimeter, electrical circuit, mounting hardware, environment and security integration all need to be considered.

01

Perimeter Structure

The wall, palisade, posts or other mounting structure must be suitable for the selected electric-fence configuration. The mounting arrangement should provide secure support and maintain the required electrical separation.

02

Conductor Layout

The number, spacing and routing of conductors should be planned around the perimeter. Corners, changes in direction, gates and other structures require particular attention.

03

Insulation & Clearances

Insulators must prevent unwanted electrical contact between the live conductors and supporting structures. Damaged or incorrectly selected insulators can result in leakage and reduced fence performance.

04

Earthing

The earthing system is a fundamental part of the electrical circuit. Earth rods, connections and cabling should be installed correctly and checked as part of ongoing system maintenance.

05

Vegetation Management

Grass, branches, creepers and other vegetation can make contact with fence conductors and cause electrical leakage. The area around the fence should therefore be kept suitably clear.

06

Gates & Access Points

Gates introduce moving sections and access requirements into the perimeter. Their design and electrical continuity need to be considered so that security is maintained without creating an impractical access arrangement.

07

Power & Backup

The energizer and associated monitoring equipment require reliable power. Backup arrangements can help maintain protection during mains interruptions, depending on the system design.

08

Monitoring & Integration

Where required, fence monitoring can be connected to an alarm system or broader security platform so that fence events can form part of the property's overall response strategy.

System Performance

What Can Reduce Fence Performance?

Even a correctly specified system can experience reduced performance if the perimeter is not maintained or if faults develop within the electrical circuit.

Vegetation Contact

Vegetation touching the conductors can create unwanted electrical leakage and reduce the effectiveness of the fence.

Loose or Damaged Connections

Poor connections, corrosion or physical damage can affect electrical continuity throughout the perimeter.

Electrical Leakage

Unwanted contact with conductive structures or damaged insulators can cause leakage and reduce available fence performance.

Poor Earthing

An inadequate or poorly maintained earth system can affect the operation of the complete fence circuit.

Environmental Exposure

Weather, moisture, dust and corrosion can affect components over time, particularly in exposed installations.

Physical Damage

Bent brackets, broken insulators or damaged conductors can create faults that require inspection and repair.

Professional Security Guidance

Build an Electric Fence That Performs Reliably

A professional electric-fence system should be designed, installed and maintained as a complete perimeter-security solution rather than as a collection of individual products.

01

Start With the Perimeter

Before selecting equipment, assess the actual perimeter. Identify walls, fences, gates, corners, changes in elevation, access points and areas where the fence may be exposed to physical or environmental interference.

Professional Tip Map the perimeter before choosing the system components.
02

Match the Energizer to the System

The energizer should be selected according to the actual fence configuration and installation conditions. Perimeter length, conductor arrangement, vegetation and potential electrical loading all need to be considered.

Professional Tip More output is not automatically the correct solution.
03

Never Ignore Earthing

The earth system is fundamental to the operation of an electric fence. Earth rods, connections and associated cabling should be installed correctly and inspected periodically.

Professional Tip A strong energizer cannot compensate for a poorly designed earth system.
04

Control Vegetation

Vegetation touching electric-fence conductors can cause leakage and reduce performance. Trees, creepers, grass and other growth should be monitored and kept away from the electrified sections.

Professional Tip Routine vegetation control is part of electric-fence maintenance.
05

Check Connections & Hardware

Loose connections, corrosion, damaged conductors and degraded insulators can affect the electrical performance of the perimeter. Hardware should be visually inspected regularly.

Professional Tip Small physical faults can become significant electrical problems if ignored.
06

Plan for Power Failures

Where continuous perimeter protection is important, the system should be evaluated for mains interruptions and appropriate backup-power requirements.

Professional Tip Test backup arrangements rather than assuming they will work when needed.
Electric Fence Safety

Safety Is Part of the System Design

Electric fencing is an active security barrier and should be treated accordingly. A professionally designed installation must take account of the property's occupants, visitors, neighbours, access points and the surrounding environment.

Warning signage, appropriate positioning, electrical separation, secure equipment and proper installation are important considerations when designing an electric-fence system.

Do not modify an energised fence

Never attempt to repair, alter, disconnect or work on an electric fence while it is energised. Isolate the system appropriately and use competent personnel for electrical work.

Essential Safety Principles

  • Use appropriate electric-fence warning signage.
  • Keep electrified conductors appropriately separated from other conductive structures.
  • Protect the energizer and associated equipment from unauthorised interference.
  • Keep vegetation and combustible material appropriately managed around the perimeter.
  • Inspect the system after physical damage, severe weather or other events that may affect the installation.
  • Use suitably qualified and competent installers for electrical and security-system work.

Electric Fence Maintenance Checklist

Regular inspection helps identify problems before they compromise the security perimeter.

Check What to Look For Recommended Action
Vegetation
Plants, branches or grass touching conductors. Clear vegetation and inspect affected sections.
Conductors
Broken, loose, sagging or damaged fence wires. Isolate the system and have damaged sections repaired.
Insulators
Cracks, damage, contamination or poor electrical isolation. Replace defective components and check the affected area.
Connections
Loose, corroded or damaged electrical connections. Inspect and repair using suitable components.
Earthing
Loose connections, damaged cables or deterioration. Inspect the earth system and test where appropriate.
Energizer
Fault indications, unusual operation or power problems. Follow the manufacturer's service procedure and obtain professional assistance where required.
Warning Signs
Missing, damaged or unreadable warning signage. Replace signage where necessary.
Backup Power
Battery condition and backup operation. Test and service according to the equipment manufacturer's recommendations.
