UNDERSTANDING CCTV

What Is CCTV?

CCTV, or Closed-Circuit Television, is a surveillance technology designed to capture, transmit, record and review video from specific areas of a property.

Cameras Capture Activity

CCTV cameras capture video from selected areas such as entrances, driveways, offices, warehouses, parking areas and perimeter zones.

Video Is Recorded

Depending on the system, footage can be recorded to a DVR, NVR or other storage platform for later review and investigation.

Footage Can Be Monitored

Modern CCTV systems can allow authorised users to view live or recorded footage through monitors, computers and compatible mobile devices.

Important: CCTV should not be selected simply by counting cameras. A properly designed surveillance system considers camera location, viewing angles, lighting, image quality, recording requirements, storage capacity, network infrastructure and the security objectives of the property.

CCTV TECHNOLOGY

How Does a CCTV System Work?

A CCTV system works as a connected surveillance chain. Cameras capture activity, video is transmitted to recording equipment, footage is stored and the system allows authorised users to view or retrieve that information when required.

01

Camera

Captures activity within the camera's field of view.

02

Transmission

Video travels through the system using suitable cabling or network infrastructure.

03

Recorder

A DVR or NVR processes and manages the incoming surveillance video.

04

Storage

Recorded footage is stored for a defined retention period.

05

Monitoring

Authorised users can view live or recorded footage when required.

The important principle: every part of the CCTV chain affects the final result. A high-resolution camera cannot compensate for poor positioning, inadequate lighting, unsuitable cabling, insufficient storage or an incorrectly specified recording system.

CCTV SYSTEM TYPES

HD CCTV or IP CCTV?

One of the first decisions when designing a CCTV system is choosing the right camera technology. HD CCTV and IP CCTV can both provide excellent surveillance, but they use different architectures and offer different advantages.

ANALOG HD

HD CCTV

HD CCTV systems transmit high-definition video from cameras to a DVR, typically using coaxial cabling with appropriate power connections.

  • Straightforward system architecture
  • Excellent option for many existing CCTV installations
  • Uses dedicated DVR recording
  • Often practical for smaller systems
NETWORK CCTV

IP CCTV

IP CCTV cameras generate digital video and transmit it across a network to an NVR or compatible recording infrastructure.

  • Digital network-based architecture
  • Excellent scalability for larger systems
  • Can support advanced analytics and intelligent features
  • Particularly useful for modern residential and commercial networks

Which is better? There is no universal answer. The correct choice depends on the property, existing infrastructure, required image quality, camera locations, network requirements, future expansion and budget. A properly specified system should be designed around those requirements rather than choosing technology based on resolution alone.

CAMERA SELECTION

Choosing the Right CCTV Camera

CCTV cameras are not interchangeable. The correct camera depends on where it will be installed, the area that needs to be covered, lighting conditions, viewing distance, environmental exposure and the level of detail required.

Bullet Cameras

A visible, directional camera commonly used for entrances, driveways, perimeter areas and outdoor surveillance where a defined viewing direction is required.

Dome Cameras

Compact cameras often selected for homes, offices, retail environments and indoor areas where a discreet, clean installation is preferred.

Turret Cameras

A flexible camera design that is widely used for residential and commercial installations, particularly where easy adjustment and reliable night performance are important.

PTZ Cameras

Pan-Tilt-Zoom cameras can remotely move across a scene and zoom in on areas of interest. They are useful for larger sites where active control and extended coverage are required.

Camera shape is only one part of the decision. Lens selection, focal length, field of view, resolution, sensor performance, infrared capability, low-light performance, weather protection and mounting position can have a greater impact on usable evidence than the camera's appearance alone.

CCTV SYSTEM DESIGN

How Many CCTV Cameras Do You Need?

The number of cameras required depends on the property, its layout and what you need each camera to accomplish. A professional CCTV system starts with identifying security-critical areas and potential blind spots.

Entrances & Doors

Main entrances, back doors, staff entrances and other access points are often priority areas. Camera positioning should provide useful views of people approaching and entering the property.

Gates & Driveways

Vehicle and pedestrian access points may require dedicated coverage. The required camera type and lens depend on the distance and level of detail required.

Perimeter Areas

Larger properties may require cameras positioned around the perimeter, boundary walls or vulnerable approaches to detect activity before an intruder reaches the main building.

Internal Areas

Offices, warehouses, passages, reception areas, stock rooms and other sensitive spaces may require dedicated cameras depending on the security risk.

A Simple Starting Point

Small Residential Focus on entrances, driveway, perimeter access and key blind spots.
Larger Residential Consider additional coverage for multiple access points and perimeter areas.
Business & Commercial Coverage should consider people, vehicles, stock, restricted areas and operational risk.

More cameras do not automatically mean better security. Poorly positioned cameras can create overlapping views while leaving critical areas unprotected. Effective CCTV design aims for meaningful coverage, appropriate image detail and as few unnecessary blind spots as practical.

RECORDING & STORAGE

DVR, NVR & CCTV Storage

Recording is one of the most important parts of a CCTV system. The recorder determines how video is managed, while storage capacity determines how much footage can be retained before older recordings are overwritten.

HD CCTV

DVR

A Digital Video Recorder receives video from compatible HD CCTV cameras and records the footage to internal storage. DVR systems are commonly used where cameras are connected using coaxial-based infrastructure.

IP CCTV

NVR

A Network Video Recorder manages digital video received from IP cameras across a network. NVR-based systems are particularly suited to modern network surveillance installations.

Camera Count

More cameras generally create greater storage requirements.

Resolution

Higher-resolution video can require more storage depending on compression settings.

Retention Period

The longer footage needs to be retained, the greater the storage requirement.

Compression

Video compression can reduce storage usage while maintaining useful image quality.

Storage should be designed around the security requirement. A system that records beautifully but overwrites important footage too quickly may not provide the evidence you need when an incident occurs. Camera count, resolution, frame rate, recording mode, compression and required retention period should all be considered when specifying storage.

IMAGE QUALITY & TECHNOLOGY

CCTV Performance Doesn't Stop at Resolution

A camera advertised with a high megapixel count is not automatically the best surveillance camera. Useful CCTV footage depends on lighting, lens selection, sensor performance, image processing, camera placement and the level of detail required.

Night Vision

Infrared illumination can help cameras capture scenes in darkness. However, effective night surveillance also depends on distance, reflective surfaces, ambient light and camera positioning.

Low-Light Performance

Some cameras are designed to produce better colour and detail in challenging lighting conditions. This can be particularly valuable around entrances, streets and commercial sites.

Resolution

Higher resolution can provide greater image detail, but the correct resolution depends on viewing distance, lens selection and what the camera needs to identify.

Smart Detection

Depending on the equipment, intelligent features may help distinguish people, vehicles or other events and reduce unnecessary alerts.

SECURITY OBJECTIVE

Detection

The camera needs to show that activity occurred in a particular area. This may be sufficient for general monitoring and perimeter awareness.

SECURITY OBJECTIVE

Identification

If the purpose is to identify a person, vehicle or specific event, the camera must be selected and positioned to provide sufficient usable detail at the required distance.

The goal is usable evidence. A CCTV system should be designed around what the customer needs to see and identify, not simply the largest resolution number on a product specification sheet.

REMOTE CCTV ACCESS

Monitor Your Property From Almost Anywhere

Modern CCTV systems can provide remote access through compatible mobile applications and network connections. This allows authorised users to view cameras, review recordings and receive selected alerts without being physically present at the property.

Live Camera View

Authorised users can view connected cameras remotely using a compatible smartphone, tablet or computer application.

Remote Playback

Depending on the recorder and network setup, authorised users may search and review stored footage remotely when an incident needs to be investigated.

Event Notifications

Compatible systems may provide notifications for configured events such as motion or intelligent detection, depending on the camera and recorder capabilities.

Multiple Locations

Business users with compatible systems can potentially manage cameras from more than one location through a centralised application.

HOW IT WORKS

CCTV Remote Access Explained

01

Cameras Capture

Cameras capture video and send it to the appropriate recording equipment.

02

Recorder Stores

The DVR or NVR records and manages the surveillance footage.

03

Network Connection

The recorder or camera system connects to the appropriate network infrastructure.

04

Authorised Access

An authorised user accesses the system through a compatible application or platform.

Remote access must be secured properly. CCTV systems connected to the internet should use strong unique credentials, appropriate user permissions, current firmware where supported and secure network configuration. Remote viewing should never mean leaving a surveillance system unnecessarily exposed to the public internet.

PROFESSIONAL INSTALLATION

A CCTV System Is Only as Good as Its Design

Professional CCTV installation involves more than mounting cameras and connecting cables. Camera positioning, lens selection, cabling, power, recording, network infrastructure and environmental conditions all affect the performance of the finished surveillance system.

STEP 01

Site Assessment

Identify entrances, vulnerable areas, perimeter zones, valuable assets, blind spots and other locations where surveillance provides meaningful security value.

STEP 02

Camera Selection

Select camera type, resolution, lens, viewing angle, night capability and environmental protection according to each location.

STEP 03

Cabling & Power

Cable routing, termination, power requirements and network infrastructure must be planned to provide reliable communication between the cameras and recording equipment.

STEP 04

Recording & Storage

The recorder and storage capacity should be selected according to camera count, recording requirements, resolution and desired retention period.

INSTALLATION QUALITY

Camera Positioning

Camera height, viewing angle and orientation should be selected carefully. A camera can have excellent specifications and still produce poor evidence if it is positioned incorrectly.

INSTALLATION QUALITY

Cable Protection

CCTV cabling should be routed and protected appropriately for the installation environment. Outdoor runs require particular attention to weather exposure and physical vulnerability.

Good CCTV installation is about engineering, not simply mounting hardware. The objective is a reliable surveillance system that produces useful footage, protects critical areas, remains maintainable and continues performing under the conditions for which it was designed.

SYSTEM SELECTION

Which CCTV System Is Right for You?

There is no single CCTV package that is ideal for every property. The right system depends on the property size, number of surveillance points, required image detail, lighting conditions, recording period, network infrastructure and future expansion.

1. Property Size

A small home may require only a few strategically positioned cameras, while larger properties can require additional cameras, longer cable runs, additional network equipment and larger storage.

2. Security Objective

Decide whether the system is primarily intended for general monitoring, incident verification, vehicle identification, facial identification, perimeter awareness or a combination of these.

3. Lighting Conditions

Daylight, darkness, street lighting, strong backlighting and changing light conditions can influence camera selection and image quality.

4. Recording Period

Determine how long recordings need to remain available. More cameras, higher resolutions and longer retention periods generally require more storage capacity.

5. Remote Viewing

If you need to monitor the property while away, the CCTV system should be selected with suitable network connectivity and secure remote-access capabilities.

6. Future Expansion

Consider whether additional cameras or other security technologies may be required later. Selecting suitable equipment from the beginning can make future expansion easier.

IMAGE QUALITY

More Megapixels Do Not Automatically Mean Better Evidence

CCTV image quality is determined by several factors working together. Resolution matters, but lens selection, sensor performance, lighting, camera positioning, compression and the distance between the camera and the subject can be equally important.

Resolution

Resolution determines the amount of visual detail captured by the camera. Higher resolutions can provide greater detail, but the benefit depends on the viewing distance and the intended purpose of the camera.

Lens Selection

The lens affects the camera's field of view and how large a subject appears in the image. Wide views cover more area, while narrower views can provide greater detail at specific distances.

Image Sensor

The image sensor converts light into the electronic information used to create the video. Sensor performance can have a significant effect on colour, detail and low-light image quality.

Lighting

Even a high-resolution camera can produce poor footage when lighting is inadequate or difficult. Backlighting, darkness, glare and rapidly changing illumination must be considered during selection.

LENS SELECTION

Wide Angle vs Narrow View

A wider field of view allows a camera to cover more area, but subjects may appear smaller. A narrower field of view covers less area while allowing the camera to concentrate more pixels on a particular zone.

PROFESSIONAL PRINCIPLE

Design for the Target

A camera intended to monitor a gate, for example, may require a different lens and mounting position from a camera intended to provide a broad overview of a large yard. CCTV design should therefore begin with the target area rather than the camera box.

CCTV Should Be Designed Around Evidence

The objective of a surveillance camera is not simply to produce an attractive live image. It should capture the information required to understand an event and, where appropriate, provide useful recorded evidence.

NIGHT SURVEILLANCE

CCTV Must Perform When the Sun Goes Down

Night-time surveillance presents different challenges from daytime monitoring. Darkness, shadows, headlights, street lighting and reflective surfaces can all affect the quality of recorded footage. Selecting the correct camera technology is therefore critical for effective 24-hour surveillance.

INFRARED

IR Night Vision

Infrared illumination allows compatible cameras to capture images in darkness without requiring visible white light. The resulting image is generally monochrome when infrared mode is active.

LOW-LIGHT COLOUR

Colour Night Vision

Some modern cameras are designed to retain colour information under suitable low-light conditions. This can provide additional visual information when colour is important to the surveillance objective.

ACTIVE ILLUMINATION

White-Light Cameras

Cameras with integrated visible-light illumination can improve colour visibility at night while also providing an active lighting deterrent. Their use should be considered carefully according to the property and surveillance environment.

LOW-LIGHT SENSOR

Starlight-Type Cameras

High-sensitivity low-light cameras are designed to extract useful image information from very limited available light. Actual performance depends on the sensor, lens and surrounding lighting conditions.

NIGHT-TIME DESIGN

What Affects Night-Time CCTV Performance?

Viewing Distance

The farther the subject is from the camera, the more important lens selection, illumination and pixel density become.

Vehicle Headlights

Bright headlights entering the camera's field of view can create challenging exposure conditions. Camera positioning and suitable image-processing features are important.

Reflective Surfaces

Walls, glass, polished vehicles and other reflective surfaces can affect night-time images and infrared performance.

Weather & Environment

Rain, dust, fog and other environmental conditions can reduce visibility. Outdoor cameras should be selected according to the environment in which they will operate.

Do Not Judge a CCTV Camera Only by Its Advertised Night-Vision Distance

Advertised illumination distances are not a guarantee that a person or vehicle will be identifiable at that distance. Real-world performance depends on the camera, lens, environment, subject movement, lighting and installation. Professional CCTV design should evaluate the actual surveillance objective.

CCTV FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About CCTV

Answers to common questions about CCTV cameras, recording, storage, night vision and choosing a surveillance system.

How many CCTV cameras do I need?

The number of cameras depends on the size and layout of the property, entrances, access points, vulnerable areas, perimeter requirements and the level of coverage required. Camera placement is generally more important than simply increasing the number of cameras.

What is the difference between CCTV and IP CCTV?

Traditional HD CCTV systems commonly transmit video through coaxial cabling to a DVR, while IP CCTV cameras transmit digital video through network infrastructure to an NVR or compatible network recording platform. The correct choice depends on the property, infrastructure and system requirements.

Can I view my CCTV cameras from my phone?

Many modern CCTV systems support remote viewing through compatible applications. Remote access depends on the recorder, camera platform, network connectivity and appropriate configuration.

How long can CCTV footage be stored?

Storage duration depends on the number of cameras, resolution, frame rate, compression, recording mode and hard-drive capacity. A professional system should be designed around the required retention period rather than choosing storage independently from the cameras.

Does CCTV work at night?

Yes. Modern CCTV cameras can use infrared illumination, low-light sensors, visible-light illumination or a combination of technologies. Night-time performance depends on the camera, lens, lighting and installation environment.

What resolution CCTV camera should I choose?

The appropriate resolution depends on the surveillance objective. General monitoring may require less detail, while applications involving people, vehicles, gates or specific identification requirements may justify higher resolution and more carefully selected lenses.
