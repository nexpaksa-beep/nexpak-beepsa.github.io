Module 7: Permanent Fencing Systems

Typical wood post service life (treated, ground-set)15–25 years
Typical insulator UV service life8–12 years
Concrete needed forCorner & gate posts under tension
Concrete generally avoided forStandard line posts

Permanent fencing is a foundation project as much as a fencing one. Corner and gate posts carrying real tension are typically set in concrete, while line posts in stable soil are usually tamped — concreting every post is rarely necessary and can trap moisture against wood posts, accelerating rot at the collar.

A common and cost-effective permanent approach for horse perimeters is a hybrid system: two or three strands of highly visible electric tape or rope mounted on the inside of a traditional wood, vinyl, or pipe fence. This adds a psychological deterrent that keeps horses from leaning, cribbing, or chewing the structural rail, dramatically extending its life without changing the outer appearance of the fence.

Longevity planning means budgeting for periodic replacement, not assuming a "permanent" fence is maintenance-free: wood posts at the ground line, insulators (which become brittle with UV exposure), and any wire or tape under constant tension all have a realistic service life that should be documented at installation so a future inspection isn't guessing at the fence's age.

Hybrid systems extend structural fence life

An electrified offset strand on the inside of a wood or vinyl fence is one of the most cost-effective retrofits for stopping leaning, cribbing and rail damage on existing perimeters.

Check yourself (not graded)

1. Why might concreting every single line post actually shorten a wood fence's life?
Concrete can hold moisture against the buried wood at the collar, accelerating rot compared to a tamped-soil setting that allows more drainage.
